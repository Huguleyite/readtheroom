Reading the returns

What the numbers mean

Boxes, not ballots

The state’s results page tracks “boxes reported,” the share of a county’s voting boxes that have turned in totals. It does not measure how many ballots are counted. A county can show most of its boxes and still have votes to add.

Why the numbers jump

Counties send results in batches, so totals rise in steps rather than along a smooth line. Alabama counts absentee ballots after the polls close at 7 p.m. Central. In a small county, one large box can move a percentage noticeably. An early lead is a snapshot, not a trend.

Leading is not winning

This page does not call races. It shows the votes counted and who is ahead. A lead with boxes outstanding can change. Results stay unofficial until certified.

Unofficial, then official

Every number here is unofficial. Each county’s canvassing board meets at noon on the second Friday after the election, November 13, to tally the returns. The state canvassing board, made up of the governor, the secretary of state, and the attorney general, certifies state and federal races within 22 days, by November 25. In a general election, a race decided by 0.5 percent or less triggers an automatic recount.

About these numbers

Results come from the Alabama Secretary of State’s unofficial election night reporting, which relays what each county submits. Each county’s probate judge is responsible for the accuracy of what the county reports. This page refreshes about every 10 minutes and can trail the state’s own site. Every figure shows the time of its last update.
