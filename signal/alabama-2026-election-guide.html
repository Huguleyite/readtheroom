Alabama 2026 Election Guide: Your Nov. 3 Ballot in Chambers County

Who is running, what the four constitutional amendments do, and every deadline from the October 19 registration cutoff to the November 25 state canvass.

June 11, 2026  ·  Updated October 15, 2026  ·  The Frequency Signal  ·  Alabama Politics

The Dates That Matter
  • October 19 — Last day to register to vote
  • October 27 — Mailed absentee applications must be received
  • October 29 — Last day to apply in person for an absentee ballot
  • November 2 — In-person absentee ballots due by close of business
  • November 3 — Election Day. Polls open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Mailed absentee ballots must arrive by noon.
  • November 10 — Provisional ballots are counted. Overseas military ballots postmarked by Election Day are accepted until noon.
  • November 13 — County canvassing boards certify results at noon
  • November 25 — Deadline for the state canvass

Governor: The Rematch

Kay Ivey is term-limited, and the race to replace her is one Alabama has seen before. Senator Tommy Tuberville won the May 19 Republican primary with about 85 percent of the vote. Former senator Doug Jones won the Democratic nomination with about 78 percent. In 2020, Tuberville took Jones’s Senate seat by roughly 20 points. Six years later, the same two names meet again, this time for the governor’s office.

U.S. Senate: The Open Seat

Tuberville’s run for governor leaves his Senate seat open, and neither party settled the nomination on May 19. Congressman Barry Moore won the Republican nomination over businessman Jared Hudson, 56 to 44 percent. On the Democratic side, attorney Everett Wess defeated businessman Dakarai Larriett 55 to 45. Moore and Wess meet November 3.

Four Constitutional Amendments

Alabama voters will also decide four statewide constitutional amendments. Each passed the Legislature with the required three-fifths vote in both chambers.

Amendment 1: Lieutenant Governor Vacancies

Requires a statewide election to fill a lieutenant governor vacancy that occurs more than 60 days before a general election. Today the seat stays empty for the rest of the term. Also lets the Legislature set the pay of the lieutenant governor and legislators.

SponsorSen. Sam Givhan (R)
Senate29–0
House101–0

Amendment 2: School System Mergers

Creates a process for neighboring county school boards to merge:

  1. The State Board of Education completes an impact study.
  2. Residents have 30 days to file an initiative, signed by 25 percent of voters in the affected systems.
  3. A merger needs a majority vote in each county.
SponsorRep. Terri Collins (R)
Senate30–0
House96–2

Amendment 3: Pledge of Allegiance and Student Prayer

Local school boards must adopt a policy for a daily Pledge of Allegiance in every public school and allow student-initiated, student-led prayer. Students can opt out of either.

SponsorRep. Reed Ingram (R)
Senate30–0
House94–3

Amendment 4: National Anthem

Local school boards must adopt policies to broadcast or sanction the first stanza of the “Star-Spangled Banner” at least once a week.

SponsorSen. Gerald Allen (R)
Senate23–5
House75–27

Mostly along party lines.

On the ballot, these appear as Statewide Amendments 1 through 4.

The Undercard

Lieutenant Governor

State party chairman John Wahl won the Republican nomination, defeating Secretary of State Wes Allen 57 to 43 percent. Wahl faces Democrat Phillip Ensler in November.

Attorney General

Katherine Robertson won the Republican nomination, defeating Jay Mitchell 55 to 45 percent. Robertson faces Democrat Jeff McLaughlin in November.

Secretary of State

Caroleene Dobson won the Republican primary outright with 66 percent of the vote. She faces Democrat Wayne Rogers in November.

Public Service Commission

Place 1 is set: Cullman County Sheriff Matt Gentry, who unseated Commissioner Jeremy Oden in the primary, faces Democrat James Gordon. Place 2 went to former state auditor Jim Zeigler, who defeated Commissioner Chip Beeker 51.34 to 48.66 percent. Democrat Sheila McNeil faces Zeigler in November. We covered why the PSC decides what you pay for power in an earlier Signal.

Other Statewide Offices

Also on your ballot: State Treasurer, Democrat Rosilyn Houston vs. Republican Young Boozer. State Auditor, Democrat Violet Edwards vs. Republican Andrew Sorrell. Commissioner of Agriculture and Industries, Democrat Ron Sparks vs. Republican Corey Hill. State Board of Education District 2, Democrat Jamie McCurry vs. Republican Tracie West. Associate Justice of the Supreme Court, Place 8, Democrat Ashleigh Meyer Dunham vs. Republican Greg Shaw. Place 7 (Brad Mendheim), Court of Civil Appeals Places 4 (Benjamin M. Bowden) and 5 (Matt Fridy), and Court of Criminal Appeals Places 4 (Mary Windom) and 5 (Riggs Walker) are Republicans with no opponent on the ballot.

Your Ballot in Chambers County

U.S. House, 3rd District

Congressman Mike Rogers, in office since 2003, faces Democrat Lee McInnis, an Auburn graduate and Army intelligence veteran.

State Senate, District 13

Democrat Jamie Forsyth faces Republican Randy Price on November 3.

State House, District 38

Hazel Floyd is back on the ballot. The 22-year-old Valley native lost February’s special election to Republican Kristin Nelson while being outspent $58,135 to $1,527.64 — every dollar of hers from individual donors. She re-qualified anyway, won May’s Democratic primary with 73.85 percent, and faces Nelson again November 3 in a district Republicans have held since 2014.

Part of Chambers County votes in House District 37, where Democrat Michelle French faces Republican Jeff Monroe.

County Offices

Partisan county offices appear on the November 3 ballot. Sheriff Jeff Nelson and Revenue Commissioner Beth Abney, both Republicans, are unopposed. For coroner, Republican Levi Richardson, a Lanett paramedic and five-year deputy coroner who won the June 16 runoff, faces Democrat Maurice Ward. Depending on where you live, your ballot also lists County Commission District 3 (John Benefield, R, unopposed) or District 5 (Sandra Calloway Crim, D, vs. Sara Crutchfield, R), and County Board of Education District 2 (Candace Lyons, D, unopposed) or District 4 (Jody Jennings, R, unopposed). Municipal officers were elected August 25 in nonpartisan races. Check the sample ballots below or at alabamavotes.gov.

How to Register and Vote

Register, confirm your registration, or find your polling place at alabamavotes.gov. The registration deadline for the general election is October 19.

Absentee ballot applications go through the Chambers County circuit clerk in LaFayette. Mailed applications must be received by October 27, and in-person applications by October 29. In-person absentee ballots are due by close of business November 2, and mailed ballots must arrive by noon on Election Day. Alabama requires a valid photo ID to vote, at the polls or by absentee ballot.
