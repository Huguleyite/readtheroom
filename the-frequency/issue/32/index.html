01 Energy & Utilities

Unbilled.

Alabama Power says it will stop charging customers for most advertising, more than two years before state law requires it.

Alabama Power announced Nov. 9 that it will stop billing customers for advertising costs, except those tied to public safety. The decision followed a request from the Alabama Public Service Commission.

The commission’s three members voted unanimously on Nov. 3, Inside Climate News reports, on a motion by President Cynthia Lee Almond. It asked the utility to exclude advertising from retail rate setting through Dec. 31, 2028. The request is voluntary.

Until now, Alabama Power could bill customers for half of its advertising that is not tied to public safety, according to Inside Climate News. A law passed this spring bars the practice starting Jan. 1, 2029, so the company will absorb those costs sooner than required. The effect on customer rates is not immediately clear, the Alabama Reflector reports.

Customers had been helping pay for the commercials telling them how reliable the service is.

Source: Alabama Reflector, Nov. 11, 2026; Inside Climate News, Nov. 7, 2026

02 Economy

Day One.

Legislative Republicans say ending the last 2% of the state grocery tax comes first in 2027, and the governor-elect wants income-tax relief next.

Republican legislative leaders say eliminating the remaining 2% state tax on groceries will be a “day-one priority” of the 2027 session. House Speaker Ledbetter and Senate President Pro Tem Garlan Gudger announced the plan Oct. 16 at a Huntsville supermarket.

The bill is drafted, and Ledbetter said he wants it on the House floor within the first few weeks. The state tax on groceries fell from 4% to 3% in September 2023 and from 3% to 2% in September 2025. For a family spending $1,000 a month on groceries, the last 2% costs $240 a year.

Tommy Tuberville, who won the governor’s race Nov. 3, wants to gradually cut income taxes. He has not given rates, amounts or a timeline. The mechanism involves the federal income tax deduction and could require voter approval. Ledbetter said he favors the idea but that any cut needs revenue to replace what the state loses. He credits economic growth.

Grocery tax repeal drew support from both sides of the governor’s race. Tuberville and Democrat Doug Jones each backed it during the campaign.

Source: Alabama Political Reporter, Oct. 19, 2026; ABC 33/40, Nov. 6, 2026; WSFA

03 State Government

Frozen.

A legislative panel holds two prison legal contracts while the $1.25 billion Elmore County facility misses its schedule.

The Legislature’s Joint Contract Review Committee held two Alabama Department of Corrections legal-services contracts Nov. 12. A hold is not a rejection, and the contracts can return. One is a $200,000 deal for an attorney advising on construction procurement and spending. The other covers the outside monitoring team in the long-running prison litigation.

Rep. Chris England, D-Tuscaloosa, asked when the Elmore County prison will be finished. A department representative acknowledged slow progress and gave no completion date. The $1.25 billion facility was slated for completion Oct. 15, against an original target of May 2026.

Ledbetter said he spoke with Tuberville, who is concerned about spending on a new facility before the first is finished. Because the Legislature appropriates the money, Ledbetter said, lawmakers need more oversight. He expects “much stronger oversight” once Tuberville takes office.

Lawmakers want a clearer view of a project whose finish line keeps moving.

Source: ABC 33/40, Nov. 12, 2026; Alabama Political Reporter, Nov. 11, 2026; Alabama Reflector, Oct. 16, 2026

04 Elections & Voting Local

Shifted.

Democrats flipped four legislative seats, and Alabama’s biggest counties moved further than Chambers did.

Democrats flipped four legislative seats Nov. 3, the first dent in Republican supermajorities in more than a decade, according to the Alabama Political Reporter. Jared Sluss and Rudolph Valentino Drake won Senate seats. Nell Brown and Allison Montgomery won House seats. Sluss beat Sen. Sam Givhan by 577 votes. Republicans now hold 74 of 105 House seats and 24 of 35 Senate seats.

The Alabama Reflector reported that Democratic ballots rose in all 67 counties. The Political Reporter found that Jefferson, Madison, Mobile, Montgomery, Tuscaloosa, Shelby, Baldwin and Lee counties together went from a 19-point Republican margin in the 2022 attorney general race to a 7-point Democratic margin. Jones carried Tuscaloosa County with about 54%, a reversal from 2022.

The east Alabama counties moved too, though Tuberville held them. In Chambers County he led Jones by 281 votes, after Gov. Kay Ivey won it by 3,071 in 2022. In Lee County he won by 1,754, against Ivey’s 15,439. In Pike County he won by 370, against Ivey’s 2,807.

Disclosure: Hazel Floyd, a candidate in House District 38, is the daughter of Sim Floyd, Jr., who publishes Read the Room.

Source: Alabama Political Reporter, Nov. 6, 2026; Alabama Reflector, Nov. 4-12, 2026; WBRC, Nov. 5, 2026

05 Local Government Local

Sworn.

Three Chambers County commissioners take their oaths, and the county engineer reports a closed bridge on County Road 92.

Three commissioners took the oath of office at the Chambers County Commission’s Nov. 12 meeting in the upstairs courtroom in LaFayette. County Attorney Skip McCoy administered the oaths. Debra Riley (District 6) returns for another term. Sara Crutchfield (District 5) succeeds David Eastridge. John Benefield (District 3) takes the seat held by James Williams. Benefield said he is looking forward to “trying to improve the conditions in Chambers County.”

County Engineer Josh Harvill reported that County Road 92 closed Veterans Day after a local logging operation raised concerns about a bridge. Repair plans are in the works. Paving is finished on County Road 79 near the Cusseta Industrial Park, and resurfacing on County Road 83 in Cusseta starts Monday, Nov. 16.

Source: Valley Times-News, Nov. 16, 2026
