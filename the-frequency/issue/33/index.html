01 Economy Local

Zoned In.

Next door in Lee County, Opelika planners back a technology district where data centers would be allowed.

The Opelika Planning Commission voted Nov. 17 to recommend creating a new Technology Overlay District and applying it to about 519 acres along Interstate 85 and West Point Parkway. Both recommendations go to the City Council. Neither vote approved a data center or named a company to build one.

The land was pitched in 2007 as Celebrate Alabama, a retail and entertainment development that was never built. The proposed district’s 21 technology-related uses include data centers. Shane Davis of developer Breland Companies told commissioners, “We do not have any pending deal with a data center,” and said a data center is among the uses the company is pursuing. Breland says it is seeking no public money to develop the site. That does not settle whether a future occupant will seek incentives.

The meeting ran more than three hours. Residents questioned water use, power demand, noise and job counts. Chair Lucinda Cannon ordered the room cleared after applause, then let residents back in. Commissioners recommended conditions, including conditional-use approval from the Planning Commission for development in the district, limits on building height and noise, residential buffers and restricted generator testing hours. A petition against the rezoning has drawn more than 5,000 signatures.

Source: Opelika Observer, Nov. 19, 2026; AL.com, Nov. 18, 2026

02 Local Government Local

Taking the Chair.

Sam Bradford will lead the Chambers County Commission, which also approved a resolution for safety work on County Road 83.

The Chambers County Commission on Nov. 23 unanimously elected District 4 Commissioner Sam Bradford chairman and District 6 Commissioner Debra Riley vice chairman. The meeting also honored Bradford for 10 years of service to the county and recognized District 2 Commissioner Doug Jones for his service.

Commissioners approved a resolution to use federal money from the Alabama Department of Transportation’s 2027 Local Road Safety Initiative on about four miles of County Road 83, from County Road 292 to State Highway 50. The project includes guardrails and safety improvements at nine curves. Last week’s issue reported that resurfacing on the road in Cusseta was set to start Nov. 16.

The commission also approved a $384,049 grant agreement with the Alabama Department of Youth Services for 2026-27, a budget amendment toward a one-man Rosco pothole patcher, and bids for road and bridge materials and services. The road resolution, grant, budget amendment and bids all passed unanimously.

Source: Valley Times-News, Nov. 25, 2026; The LaFayette Sun, Nov. 24, 2026

03 Education Local

Moved Up.

LaFayette High scores a 73 after three straight D-range report cards.

LaFayette High School earned an overall 73 on the 2025-26 state report card. It scored 68 on the 2023 card, 63 in 2024 and 67 in 2025. The C lifts the school out of the D-and-F range that puts a campus on Alabama’s priority list, often called the failing schools list. Seventh and eighth graders joined the school in 2025-26, so the new score covers a different mix of students than earlier scores did.

Year over year, academic achievement rose from 20.48 to 38.03, academic growth from 70 to 79.7 and the graduation rate from 84.62% to 86.79%. That rate is still short of 2024’s 91.84%. Math proficiency rose from 4.17% to 6.3%. Two other measures slipped. Chronic absenteeism, where higher is worse, rose from 12.04 to 15.16, and college and career readiness fell from 73.08 to 71.7.

Chambers County Schools Superintendent Sharon Weldon highlighted gains at both LaFayette and Valley high schools when she presented the results to the school board Nov. 18. LaFayette’s report card can go on the refrigerator.

Source: Alabama State Department of Education state and federal report cards, 2023–2026; Valley Times-News, Nov. 20, 2026; The LaFayette Sun, Nov. 17, 2026; AL.com via Yahoo, Nov. 16, 2026

04 State Government

Warrant Wanted.

Sen. Arthur Orr wants rules on who can search license plate camera data and how long police can keep it.

State Sen. Arthur Orr, R-Decatur, plans a 2027 bill to regulate license plate readers, the cameras that record passing vehicles’ plates. His proposal would require police to get a warrant to search the data except in urgent cases, penalize officers who misuse it and shorten how long it is kept. Alabama’s current rules cap retention at five years, with exceptions for active investigations and pending cases.

Orr has tried before. His 2021 bill passed the Senate and died in the House.

Source: Alabama Reflector, Nov. 24, 2026; WAFF, Nov. 24, 2026; WTVY, Sept. 1, 2026; Alabama Administrative Code 265-X-6-.06

05 State Government

No Parade.

Tommy Tuberville will take office Jan. 18 without the traditional inaugural parade.

Gov.-elect Tommy Tuberville’s transition committee, citing low past attendance and security costs, said Nov. 25 that his inauguration will have no parade. Mobile businessman Elliot Maisel chairs the committee. The inauguration falls on Martin Luther King Jr. Day, Jan. 18, and the committee says it plans to arrange a donation to cover civil rights museum visits that day.

Mobile and Huntsville will hold celebrations the week before. Sponsors will help cover costs, and free event tickets will be offered through a public lottery. The lottery process is set to begin next month.

“We’re not coming to Montgomery to throw ourselves a big party. We’re coming to work,” Tuberville said. He plans to sign his first executive orders immediately after the ceremony.

Source: Alabama Reflector, Nov. 25, 2026; WAKA, Nov. 25, 2026
